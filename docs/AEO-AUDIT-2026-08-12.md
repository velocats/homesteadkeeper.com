# Homestead Keeper AEO/GEO Audit

**Site:** https://homesteadkeeper.com/  
**Audit date:** August 12, 2026  
**Scope:** Live rendered homepage plus 16 priority pages; live `robots.txt`, sitemap index, and child sitemap; local Astro templates and content data; publicly visible search results and App Store listing.  
**Standard:** Findings are labeled **Confirmed**, **Inference**, or **Requires account/tool access**. Scores are diagnostic, not promises of rankings or citations.

## A. Executive summary

### Overall assessment

Homestead Keeper has a technically sound, unusually clear product website. The sampled pages are server-rendered, text-rich, canonicalized, internally linked, mobile-oriented, and supported by a clean sitemap. The homepage, product tour, feature pages, use-case pages, FAQ, pricing, and workflows consistently explain what the app does. Search engines already surface multiple pages, which confirms practical crawl and indexing access.

Its main AEO constraint is **source-worthiness, not crawlability**. Most indexable pages are commercial product descriptions written in the brand voice. They explain features well, but they do not establish a named publisher/developer, demonstrate a documented editorial process, cite primary references, or provide much original knowledge that another answer engine would need to quote. Several use-case pages also follow a highly repeated template. They are useful landing pages, but without firsthand examples or unique evidence they risk feeling interchangeable.

The best strategy is therefore to preserve the current technical foundation while adding a small number of genuinely useful, experience-backed resources: sample record systems, downloadable checklists, annotated app workflows, field-tested templates, and transparent “how we built/tested this” notes. AEO should be treated as an extension of people-first SEO, not as special AI markup. Google explicitly says there is no special AEO schema and that `llms.txt` does not affect Google Search visibility.

### Three strongest AEO advantages

1. **Clear entity and product proposition.** The homepage immediately says that Homestead Keeper combines tasks, animals, gardens, inventory, maintenance, and property records on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Privacy, offline use, and optional iCloud sync are stated consistently.
2. **Strong technical accessibility.** Live `robots.txt` allows all crawlers, references a working sitemap index, and the child sitemap lists 40 indexable URLs. Sampled pages have self-referencing canonicals, unique titles/descriptions, one H1, visible text, and no dependence on client-side JavaScript for core content.
3. **Useful topical architecture.** Feature, use-case, workflow, tutorial, pricing, FAQ, privacy, and support sections form understandable paths. The product tour is deep (about 1,900 words), and screenshots have descriptive alt text. Breadcrumb structured data is present across internal pages.

### Three largest risks

1. **Weak publisher and experience proof.** The App Store identifies the provider as Quiet Tools LLC, while website markup identifies the organization only as Homestead Keeper. There is no prominent About page, named developer/author, editorial policy, corrections policy, or explanation of firsthand homestead expertise.
2. **Commercial content outweighs citable knowledge.** The site describes the app thoroughly but has little independent, non-promotional material that answers broader homestead-management questions. An answer engine can summarize many pages without needing to cite them.
3. **Template repetition and overlapping intent.** Feature and use-case pages for gardens, animals, inventory, task tracking, and maintenance repeat similar language and CTAs. Their distinctions are understandable, but unique evidence, examples, screenshots, and decision guidance need strengthening to avoid near-commodity pages.

### Five highest-priority actions

1. Publish an **About Homestead Keeper / Quiet Tools LLC** page and connect the brand, legal publisher, developer, support contact, App Store listing, and product through visible copy and `Organization`/`SoftwareApplication` identifiers.
2. Add **firsthand evidence blocks** to priority use-case pages: annotated screenshots, a realistic example record set, limitations, and a short “how this workflow was tested” note.
3. Build 4–6 **source-worthy cornerstone resources**, beginning with homestead record-keeping, seasonal maintenance, chicken records, garden-season records, and inventory systems. Give away useful templates rather than publishing dozens of thin question pages.
4. Strengthen page relationships with **visible breadcrumbs**, contextual links between each use case and its exact workflow, and clearer parent/child hub links.
5. Establish measurement in Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance, privacy-compatible analytics, and server/CDN logs; record a baseline before editorial changes.

## D. Page-level review

The recommended openings are intentionally 40–70 words and should be adapted only if every claim remains accurate.

### 1. Homepage — `/`

- **Primary intent:** Understand and evaluate the app.
- **Likely question:** What is Homestead Keeper, and is it suitable for my property?
- **Strengths:** Immediate product definition, Apple platforms, privacy/offline differentiators, reviews, App Store link, strong application schema.
- **Missing/unclear:** Publisher identity, who built it, explicit fit/non-fit criteria, a compact feature comparison.
- **Suggested title:** `Homestead Management & Record-Keeping App | Homestead Keeper`
- **Suggested H1:** `Manage homestead records, chores, animals, gardens, and maintenance in one app.`
- **Opening:** “Homestead Keeper is a local-first record-keeping app for backyard homesteads, hobby farms, and rural properties. It brings chores, animals, garden beds, inventory, equipment maintenance, reminders, and reports together on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. No Homestead Keeper account is required, and optional iCloud sync keeps records available across your Apple devices.”
- **Structure:** Add “Who it is for,” “What it replaces,” and “Who publishes it”; link exact problems to use cases.
- **Schema:** Sitewide `Organization` + `WebSite`; `SoftwareApplication` with publisher, official App Store URL, supported OS, current offers, and verified reviews.

### 2. Product tour — `/more-info/`

- **Primary intent:** Detailed evaluation.
- **Likely question:** What can Homestead Keeper track, and how does it work day to day?
- **Strengths:** Deepest page, concrete features, screenshots, workflows, comparison, reviews, pricing, full entity graph.
- **Missing/unclear:** Page is long and covers several intents; no contents navigation or explicit limitations summary.
- **Suggested title:** `Homestead Keeper Product Tour: Features, Workflows & Screenshots`
- **Suggested H1:** `See how Homestead Keeper organizes a working homestead.`
- **Opening:** “Homestead Keeper organizes the mixed records of a backyard homestead, hobby farm, or rural property in one Apple app. This tour shows how chores, animals, garden layouts, inventory, equipment, reminders, calendars, and reports connect—from recording work in the field to reviewing seasonal history and exporting records when needed.”
- **Structure:** Add linked contents, “best for/not designed for,” and dated product-version note.
- **Schema:** Existing graph is appropriate; add `mainEntity` reference from `WebPage` if using a page node.

### 3. Features hub — `/features/`

- **Primary intent:** Compare capabilities.
- **Likely question:** Which homestead-management features are included?
- **Strengths:** Clear eight-area summary and screenshots.
- **Missing/unclear:** Sparse links to all child feature pages; little distinction between Free and Pro.
- **Suggested title:** `Homestead Keeper Features: Animals, Gardens, Inventory & More`
- **Suggested H1:** `Homestead management features for animals, gardens, property, and daily work.`
- **Opening:** “Homestead Keeper combines eight core record-keeping areas in one local-first app: dashboards, places and items, field logs, reminders, production, garden layouts, photos and documents, and reports. Records stay on your Apple devices, with optional iCloud sync across iPhone, iPad, and Mac.”
- **Structure:** Add feature matrix with platform/tier availability and direct child links.
- **Schema:** `CollectionPage` with `hasPart` links is defensible; keep breadcrumbs.

### 4. Use-cases hub — `/use-cases/`

- **Primary intent:** Find the best fit by job/persona.
- **Likely question:** Can Homestead Keeper handle the kind of homestead I manage?
- **Strengths:** Broad audience segmentation and descriptive links.
- **Missing/unclear:** No decision aid; text clusters can overlap.
- **Suggested title:** `Homestead Keeper Use Cases for Gardens, Animals & Rural Property`
- **Suggested H1:** `Choose the Homestead Keeper setup that matches your work.`
- **Opening:** “Homestead Keeper supports mixed homesteads as well as focused needs such as chicken records, garden planning, seasonal chores, equipment maintenance, pantry inventory, and rural-property care. Choose the use case closest to your work to see the relevant records, screenshots, workflows, limitations, and related features.”
- **Structure:** Add a decision table: user, main records, best workflow, important limitation.
- **Schema:** `CollectionPage` with `hasPart`; breadcrumbs.

### 5. Workflows hub — `/workflows/`

- **Primary intent:** Learn the app through tasks.
- **Likely question:** How do I complete common jobs in Homestead Keeper?
- **Strengths:** Real screenshots and task-based organization.
- **Missing/unclear:** No consistent outcome/prerequisite/time metadata; unfinished workflow is mentioned publicly even though its page is noindexed.
- **Suggested title:** `Homestead Keeper How-To Workflows and Illustrated Guides`
- **Suggested H1:** `Complete common Homestead Keeper tasks step by step.`
- **Opening:** “These illustrated workflows show how to set up and use Homestead Keeper for real work, including daily review, garden layouts, animal records, maps, calendars, reports, production, and harvests. Each guide should state what you will accomplish, what records you need first, and the exact steps shown in the current app version.”
- **Structure:** Standardize outcome, prerequisites, duration, version, steps, and troubleshooting.
- **Schema:** `CollectionPage`; use `HowTo` only on complete guides that visibly contain the required steps and assets.

### 6. Tutorials — `/tutorials/`

- **Primary intent:** Find video instruction.
- **Likely question:** Is there a video showing how to use this feature?
- **Strengths:** Clear categories and focused topics.
- **Missing/unclear:** Thin text, no transcripts, no video dates/durations in observed content, no `VideoObject`.

- **Missing/unclear:** Currency/region qualification and last price verification date.
- **Suggested title:** `Homestead Keeper Pricing: Free, Monthly, Yearly & Lifetime`
- **Suggested H1:** `Start free, then choose monthly, yearly, or lifetime Pro.`
- **Opening:** “Homestead Keeper is free for up to 20 items. Pro unlocks unlimited items, supported PDF and CSV exports, backup and restore, QR labels, documents, and advanced schedules. U.S. App Store prices shown are $2.99 monthly, $19.99 yearly, or $39.99 lifetime; regional prices and taxes may vary in Apple’s store.”
- **Structure:** Add last-verified date, regional caveat, and comparison table.
- **Schema:** Reference the same `SoftwareApplication` offers; do not create conflicting prices across pages.

## G. Sample improvements

### Sample 1: Homepage hero

**Current pattern:** Benefit headline followed by a product summary.

**Answer-first rewrite:**

> Homestead Keeper is a local-first record-keeping app for backyard homesteads, hobby farms, and rural properties. Track chores, animals, garden beds, inventory, equipment maintenance, reminders, and reports on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. No Homestead Keeper account is required, and optional iCloud sync keeps records current across your Apple devices.

Follow with a short “Best for / Not designed for” comparison so users and answer systems can classify the product accurately.

### Sample 2: Chicken records

**Current pattern:** Several descriptive sections about production, care, and the rest of the homestead.

**Answer-first rewrite:**

> For a small chicken flock, start with one flock profile, recurring care reminders, a daily egg-production entry, feed inventory, and a coop maintenance record. Use individual bird profiles only when you need bird-specific health, breeding, pedigree, or identification history. This keeps routine flock records simple without losing detail when one bird needs closer tracking.

Follow with an annotated sample showing exactly which records belong to the flock, individual bird, coop, and inventory.

### Sample 3: Garden-season records

**Current pattern:** General explanation of layouts, plantings, harvests, and reports.

**Answer-first rewrite:**

> A useful garden-season record connects four things: where a crop was planted, when it was planted, what happened during the season, and what was harvested. In Homestead Keeper, a bed layout provides the location, planting records capture crop and timing, field notes preserve soil or pest observations, and harvest entries make the season comparable with future years.

Follow with one crop example from bed planning through the garden-season report.

## Measurement plan

### What is publicly confirmed

- Search engines can crawl and surface the site.
- The live sitemap lists 40 indexable URLs.
- Multiple Homestead Keeper pages appear in public search results.
- The App Store and Quiet Tools website corroborate the product and publisher.

### What requires account access

- Google impressions/clicks, query coverage, AI-feature visibility, indexing reasons, and rich-result status.
- Bing citations, grounding queries, intents, topics, and citation share.
- Referral sessions and conversions from AI/search systems.
- Crawler request frequency and response behavior in server/CDN logs.
- Real-user Core Web Vitals.

### Recommended dashboard

Track monthly by landing-page group:

1. Search clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, and unique nonbranded queries.
2. Indexed vs submitted URLs and exclusions.
3. Rich-result/structured-data validity.
4. Bing AI citations, grounding queries, topics, and citation share.
5. AI referrals using referrer/source where available; keep an “unknown/direct” caveat.
6. App Store outbound clicks, planner engagement, tutorial completion, and support conversions.
7. Manual citation sample for 20 stable questions, checked monthly using a documented locale/account state.
8. Accuracy rate: whether the generated answer describes pricing, privacy, platform support, and capabilities correctly.

Do not treat citation count alone as success. A cited page should also produce accurate representation, qualified visits, or measurable product discovery.

## Platform guidance used

- Google, [Guide to optimizing for generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide): standard crawl, index, content, and page-experience practices remain foundational; no special AEO schema is required; Google ignores `llms.txt` for Search visibility.
- Google, [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features): pages must be indexed and eligible for snippets; existing SEO fundamentals apply.
- Google, [Structured data introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data): structured data helps systems understand content and may enable supported search features but does not guarantee display.
- Bing, [AI Performance](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/ai-performance-9f8e7d6c): use citation, grounding-query, intent, topic, and citation-share reporting where available.
