# Seasonal Front Matter

Voice: human, no em-dashes (spec §0). This is planner copy, ready for layout.

## My Homestead at a Glance

Fill this in once at the start of the season and the rest of the planner hangs off it. You'll
want your homestead name, your growing zone, your average last spring frost and first fall
frost, and how many days that gives you between them. Add a few notes about your place too,
like elevation or the spots that always frost first. Jot down your Places while you're here
(the barn, the garden, the pasture, the root cellar) so everything else has somewhere to point.

Once you know your frost dates, you can set them one time in Homestead Keeper and it will work
out the seed starting, transplant, and harvest windows for every Crop Planting on its own.

## The Homestead Year

This is the heart of the planner. It walks through the year by season instead of by month,
because a homestead in Vermont and one in Georgia are on completely different clocks. Everything
is written around your own frost dates, so it lands right wherever you are. Each season gathers
up what's happening across the whole place, in the same words the app uses.

**Early Spring** (roughly six to ten weeks before your last frost)
The quiet, hopeful stretch. Start your slow crops indoors, like onions, peppers, and tomatoes.
Dig out last year's seeds, see what's still good, and note what you're short on in Inventory.
Get the dormant pruning finished in the Orchard and put down a dormant oil spray before the
trees wake up. If you're growing the flock, this is when to order chicks and sketch out your
spring breeding. Keep half an eye on the forecast for a late Frost Event.

**Late Spring** (right around your last frost)
Now it speeds up. Harden off and transplant, direct sow your warm weather crops, and get your
first Crop Plantings in the ground. In the Orchard, spray at petal fall and plant any new Fruit
Trees. This is baby season too, so you'll be logging births (Born), moving animals out to
pasture (Moved), and staying ahead of parasites. Give the hives their first real spring
inspection, and eat down last season's stores while you're at it.

**Summer**
Head down, hands busy. Keep sowing in succession, keep the water on, and start bringing in the
first Harvest. Watch for trouble and write down any Pest Sighting or Deer Pressure. This is when
the preserving starts in earnest, with canning, freezing, dehydrating, and fermenting. The
animals hit their stride too, so your Production climbs, and it's a good time to plan fall
breeding. Slip in a mid season service on the mower and haying gear while the weather lets you.

**Late Summer**
The big push. Preserve everything that's coming in, get your fall crops going, and save seed
from your best plants. On the animal side you're weaning (Weaned) and making your keep or sell
calls (Sold). The Orchard fruit comes in now, so weigh your yields. When the shelves fill up,
count what you've actually put by (Items Stored).

**Fall** (before your first frost)
Time to batten down. Bring in the last harvest before that first Frost Event, put the Growing
Areas to bed, and get your cover crops in. Breed for spring births and stock up on Feed before
the price climbs. Cut, split, and stack your firewood, and walk the trails before the snow flies.
Winterize your water lines, your generator, and your buildings. And take an afternoon to refresh
your water, food, and fuel, and actually test the Generator Plan.

**Winter**
Rest, plan, and fix. Look back at how the year went and lay out next season's Crop Plantings
while it's fresh in your mind. Get the off season maintenance done on your equipment. Keep the
animals warm and watered, and mind the due dates for anything bred to kid or lamb in spring. Do
a deep Inventory count. And run through your storm readiness, including your Evacuation Note and
your contact list.

No hard frost where you live? Anchor to your wet and dry seasons or your local planting windows
instead, and this same rhythm still works.

The paper version is here so you can see the whole year at once. In Homestead Keeper, this same
rhythm turns into dated Upcoming Work and gentle reminders, so you're not the one keeping the
whole calendar in your head.

## This Month on the Homestead

Leave the month and year blank so you can print a fresh page any time. Up top, jot your focus
and a couple of goals. Then there's room for what's due (Upcoming Work), a quick checklist by
hub (Garden, Animals, Orchard, Food & Pantry, Inventory, and whatever else you keep), and a
small weather log for rainfall, anything notable, and your frost watch. Leave yourself space for
a running Field Log and notes, a tally of your harvests and little wins, and a box to check once
it's saved in the app. It lines up with the app's month view, so nothing feels foreign when you
switch over.

## Frost-Date and Season Worksheet

This is the one page that does the math for the folks not using the app. Write down your zone,
your average last and first frost, and the number of days between them. There's a short note on
how to find those, usually your local extension office or a frost date lookup by zip code. Then
a simple count back table: your indoor sow date is your last frost minus however many weeks a
crop needs, with blank rows to fill in your own. Do it by hand right here, or let Homestead
Keeper handle the counting for you.
